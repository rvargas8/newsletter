📘 Topic 1: Understand Multiplication and Division of Whole Numbers

Overview

Topic 1 focuses on developing your child's understanding of multiplication and division.

Multiplication as Equal Groups

Equal-group situations build on the connection between addition and multiplication — any equal groups can be described with repeated addition or with multiplication. Your child will also use skip counting on a number line and arrays (objects arranged in rows and columns) to multiply, and will use multiplication to compare two quantities.

3 bags with 8 goldfish in each: 8 + 8 + 8 = 24, or 3 × 8 = 24

Division as Sharing and Grouping

Your child will explore two kinds of division: sharing a total equally among a known number of groups to find how many are in each group, and splitting a total into equal groups of a known size to find how many groups there are. Your child will also learn how multiplication and division facts are related to each other (a "fact family").

3 × 10 = 30, so 30 ÷ 3 = 10 and 30 ÷ 10 = 3

Connect the Math at Home

Look for equal groups at home and when you're out with your child, such as pairs of socks, tires on cars, or paper towel rolls in a package. Ask your child to find the total using repeated addition or multiplication — for example, "Each of the 3 cars parked here has 4 tires. How many tires are there in all?" Do the same for division: if you're sharing food among a known number of people, ask how many items each person will get; if you know how many items you have and how many make a serving, ask how many people you can serve.
